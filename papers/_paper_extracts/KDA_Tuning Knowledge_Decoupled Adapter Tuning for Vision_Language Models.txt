KDA-Tuning: Knowledge-Decoupled Adapter 
Tuning for Vision-Language Models 
Zhengdong Zhou(B), Chenhao Ding, and Qilong Xue 
School of Software, Xi’an Jiaotong University, Xi’an, China 
3123358165@stu.xjtu.edu.cn 
Abstract. Pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) have achieved remark-
able performance across various downstream tasks. To further enhance the perfor-
mance of VLMs on downstream tasks, adapter tuning has emerged as an efﬁcient 
approach, enabling effective adaptation with minimal parameter updates. How-
ever, existing methods are susceptible to overﬁtting on task-speciﬁc data distribu-
tions, resulting in suboptimal performance. To address this challenge, we propose 
a novel framework, knowledge-decoupled adapter tuning (KDA-Tuning), which 
efﬁciently adapts VLMs to downstream tasks while preserving generalizability. 
KDA-Tuning employs a knowledge-decoupled adapter with a dual-branch struc-
ture consisting of one branch for general knowledge and another for task-speciﬁc 
knowledge, avoiding conﬂicts between their optimization. Additionally, we intro-
duce corresponding supervision, including the general knowledge retention loss 
to encourage the models to preserve general knowledge, and the task-speciﬁc 
knowledge constraint loss to enhance cross-modal alignment for distinguish-
able feature representations. Extensive experiments demonstrate that KDA-Tuning 
outperforms existing methods on typical benchmarks. 
Keywords: Knowledge-decoupled · Adapter Tuning · Vision-language Models 
1 Introduction 
Pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) [1–4] have demonstrated remarkable gener-
alization capabilities. To further enhance their performance on downstream tasks, recent 
studies focus on efﬁcient tuning paradigms, primarily categorized into prompt tuning 
[5–10] adapter tuning [ 11, 12]. Despite the great progress in efﬁcient tuning, exist-
ing methods still face several challenges. Methods like CoOp [ 5] and DAPT [ 9] face 
an optimization conﬂict: overﬁtting to downstream data distributions compromises the 
model’s ability to generalize to unseen tasks. Moreover, methods such as CLIP-Adapter 
[
11] rely on rigid residual connections with ﬁxed fusion ratios, necessitating extensive 
experiments for optimal settings across tasks, thus limiting ﬂexibility. 
To address these challenges, we propose knowledge-decoupled adapter tuning 
(KDA-Tuning), a novel framework that explicitly decouples general and task-speciﬁc 
knowledge in adaptation of VLMs. Our key insight is that latent VLM representations

2 Related Work 
Recent advances in VLMs, exempliﬁed by CLIP [ 1], ALIGN [ 2], FLIP [ 3] and Flo-
rence [ 4] have revolutionized multimodal representation learning through large-scale 
pretraining on image-text pairs. These models learn universal semantic alignments that 
enable strong zero-shot transfer across diverse vision-language tasks. Traditional full-
parameter tuning becomes impractical for large VLMs due to computational costs and 
overﬁtting risks in few-shot settings. Recent efforts explore parameter-efﬁcient tuning 
through two primary paradigms, prompt tuning and adapter tuning. Prompt tuning injects 
learnable text prompts into the encoder to guide task adaptation. CoOp [
5] pioneers con-
tinuous prompt optimization for CLIP , while CoCoOp [6] enhances instance adaptabil-
ity via image-conditioned prompts. Subsequent works [ 7–10] improve prompt design. 
However, prompt tuning requires gradient propagation through the entire text encoder, 
incurring substantial computational overhead. Adapter tuning attaches lightweight mod-
ules to frozen VLM encoders. CLIP-Adapter [
11] introduces residual MLPs to reﬁne 
visual features, and Tip-Adapter [ 12] augments CLIP with a cached feature bank for 
nearest-neighbor classiﬁcation.

3.2 Knowledge-Decoupled Adapter 
Knowledge-decoupled adapters are the fundamental component of KDA-Tuning, 
consisting of a dual-branch structure with a general branch and a task-speciﬁc 
branch. Knowledge-decoupled adapters explicitly decouple knowledge with indepen-
dent branches to avoid conﬂicts during the optimization of general and task-speciﬁc rep-
resentations. Then, knowledge is dynamically fused to perform classiﬁcation predictions, 
enabling the model to adaptively determine the optimal optimization pathway.

visual and textual branches, which share a similar structure, as shown in Fig. 2(a). Taking 
the visual adapter Av(·) as an example, it consists of a general branch Gv(·) and a task-
speciﬁc branch Sv(·), , both implemented as lightweight multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs).

Task-Speciﬁc Knowledge Constraint Loss. VLMs align visual and textual representa-
tions of the same class in the latent semantic space via contrastive learning. After training 
with large-scale image-text pairs, textual embeddings can be approximated as the centers 
of corresponding visual features in the embedding space, effectively capturing category 
comprehensive semantic information. However, due to the limited training samples in 
downstream tasks, the signiﬁcant semantic gap between the visual and textual modalities

Datasets and Benchmarks. We comprehensively evaluate KDA-Tuning across two 
critical benchmarks: few-shot learning and domain generalization. For few-shot learn-
ing, we follow standard protocols, conducting [1, 2, 4, 8, 16]-shot experiments on 11 
diverse downstream datasets, including ImageNet, StanfordCars, Caltech101, UCF101, 
Flowers102, Food101, DTD, EuroSA T, FGVCAircraft, OxfordPets, and SUN397. This 
benchmark evaluates model adaptability under limited supervision by training on K-shot 
samples per class and testing on full test sets. To evaluate domain generalization, Ima-
geNet serves as the source domain, with testing conducted on four distribution-shifted 
datasets: ImageNet-V2, -S, -A, and -R. This benchmark examines the model’s resilience 
to domain changes while ensuring category-level semantic alignment. 
Comparison Methods. We compare our KDA-Tuning with several state-of-the-art 
(SOTA) methods, including Zero-shot CLIP [ 1], CoOp [ 5], CLIP-Adapter [ 11], DAPT 
[9], Maple [7], TIP-Adapter-F [12], and TCP [10]. To ensure fair comparisons, we follow 
the same settings as the original implementations, including feature backbone, prompt 
length, and learning rate. For Zero-shot CLIP , we evaluate the pre-trained CLIP directly 
without additional training. CLIP-Adapter and TIP-Adapter-F are implemented with the 
ViT-B/16 visual encoder, conﬁgured according to ofﬁcial settings. 
Implementation Details. We utilize the ViT-B/16 [14] and transformer [ 10] as visual 
and textual encoder of CLIP respectively, which outputs features with D = 1024 chan-
nels. Margin tightness η is set to 2. All experiments are repeated three times on NVIDIA 
RTX 3090 GPUs, and the average results are reported.

Few-Shot Learning. As shown in Fig. 3, KDA-Tuning achieves superior few-shot per-
formance across 11 datasets under [1, 2, 4, 8, 16]-shot settings, reaching SOTA average 
accuracy of [70.36%, 74.43%, 77.39%, 80.67%, 82.91%]. Take representative settings 
with very few samples (1-shot), moderate samples (4-shot), and relatively more sam-
ples (16-shot) as examples, KDA-Tuning surpasses existing methods consistently. These 
results highlight KDA-Tuning’s balanced optimization of task-speciﬁc adaptation and 
generalization preservation, validating its design effectiveness.

the model on 4 sub-datasets. As summarized in Table 1, while KDA-Tuning marginally

These results validate that our dual-branch knowledge decoupling design effectively 
enhances vision-language models’ generalization capabilities, particularly in balancing 
task-speciﬁc adaptation with cross-domain robustness.

that joint multimodal feature alignment yields complementary beneﬁts.

5 Conclusion 
In this paper, we propose KDA-Tuning, a novel tuning framework that enhances VLMs 
adaptation by explicitly decoupling general and task-speciﬁc knowledge via dual-branch 
adapters. By dynamically fusing decoupled representations and enforcing specialized 
supervision, KDA-Tuning avoids conﬂicts between the optimization, enabling models 
to retain generalizability while adapting to task-speciﬁc data distributions. Extensive 
experiments validate the effectiveness of KDA-Tuning in transferring VLMs.
