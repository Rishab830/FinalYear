--- PAPER BRIEF ---

TITLE: WildFireVQA: A Large-Scale Radiometric Thermal VQA Benchmark for Aerial Wildfire Monitoring

AUTHORS: Niloufar Alipour Talemi, Mobin Habibpour, John Spodnik, Camren J. Khoury, Fatemeh Afghah
INSTITUTION: Clemson University
PUBLICATION: arXiv:2604.20190v1 [cs.CV] 22 Apr 2026

MAIN CONTRIBUTION:
Introduces WildFireVQA, a large-scale visual question answering (VQA) benchmark specifically designed for aerial wildfire monitoring that integrates RGB imagery with radiometric thermal data. The benchmark contains 6,097 RGB-thermal samples with 34 questions each, yielding 207,298 multiple-choice questions spanning wildfire-specific reasoning tasks.

KEY INNOVATIONS:
1. First wildfire-specific VQA benchmark integrating radiometric thermal data with RGB imagery
2. Provides radiometric thermal TIFFs with per-pixel temperature values (not just color-mapped visualizations)
3. Comprehensive question taxonomy covering 6 task categories: presence/detection, classification, distribution/segmentation, localization/direction, cross-modal reasoning, and flight planning
4. Sensor-driven deterministic labeling approach combining MLLM-based annotation with physical temperature thresholds
5. Multi-level consistency verification: intra-frame cross-question logic checks and inter-frame ORB-based visual similarity matching

DATASET DETAILS:
- 6,097 RGB-thermal sample pairs from FLAME 3 dataset
- Three prescribed burns: Sycan Marsh, Willamette, and Shoetank
- Three aligned modalities per sample: RGB image, color-mapped thermal visualization, radiometric thermal TIFF
- 34 questions per frame (6 binary, 28 multiple-choice)
- Total: 207,298 question-answer pairs
- Supports temperature-grounded reasoning with per-pixel temperature measurements

QUESTION CATEGORIES:
1. Presence and Detection (8 questions): Fire/smoke/flame presence, structures, fuel breaks, isolated heat sources, emergency vehicle counting
2. Classification (6 questions): Fire behavior, vegetation type, moisture level, forest canopy density, fuel type, accessibility
3. Distribution and Segmentation (8 questions): Hotspot spatial distribution, fuel continuity, intensity consistency, vegetation coverage, smoke obstruction, temperature thresholds
4. Localization and Direction (4 questions): Hotspot location, vegetation density location, smoke plume origin, structure location
5. Cross-Modal Reasoning (4 questions): Canopy obstruction, observability limitations, smoke obstruction, hotspot temperature
6. Flight Planning (4 questions): Camera viewing angle, flight altitude, safety risk assessment, UAV movement obstacles

ANNOTATION METHODOLOGY:
- Primary annotation using Gemini 2.5 Pro with RGB, thermal visualization, and radiometric statistics (min, max, std, % above 200°C and 400°C)
- Three-tier approach: (i) deterministic sensor-driven labeling for physically grounded questions, (ii) manual verification for complex questions, (iii) MLLM annotation for visually straightforward questions
- Sensor-driven methods: UAV altitude estimation using GPS+SRTM, thermal hotspot detection (200°C threshold with altitude-aware size filtering), spatial distribution via PCA-based linearity analysis, intensity consistency using robust coefficient of variation

CONSISTENCY VERIFICATION:
- Intra-frame: Cross-question logic constraints (e.g., "no fire" implies no hotspots, flames, or fire-base visibility)
- Inter-frame: ORB feature matching with homography-based geometric verification to detect near-duplicate frames and enforce label consistency

EVALUATION PROTOCOL:
- Models tested: LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, InternVL2-8B, MiniCPM-V2
- Three input settings: RGB-only, Thermal-only, with/without retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
- RAG provides compact radiometric summary: min/max/std temperature, % pixels above 200°C and 400°C
- Zero-shot evaluation to assess out-of-the-box generalization

KEY FINDINGS:
1. RGB remains strongest modality across all models (54.76% best accuracy - Qwen3-VL)
2. Retrieved thermal context helps stronger MLLMs (Qwen3-VL, InternVL2) but not weaker ones (MiniCPM-V2)
3. RAG gains concentrated on temperature-related questions: +10.07% for Qwen3-VL on temp-related subset vs. -0.36% on remaining questions
4. Specific gains: CMR4 (hottest part temperature) +29.72%, CL1 (fire behavior) +18.76%
5. Models ranked: Qwen3-VL-8B (54.76%) > LLaVA-v1.6-7B (52.68%) > MiniCPM-V2 (49.51%) > InternVL2-8B (47.66%)
6. Large performance gap vs. random accuracy (29.03%), indicating task difficulty

TECHNICAL DETAILS:
- Hotspot detection: 200°C threshold, 8-connected components, altitude-aware ground sampling distance (GSD) for physical area calculation
- Minimum hotspot size: 0.75m radius, 5 pixels
- Spatial distribution criteria: Linear (PCA eigenvalue ratio ≥0.90, max distance >20m), Concentrated (max pairwise distance ≤4×equivalent radius), otherwise Scattered
- Intensity consistency: robust coefficient of variation using MAD, threshold 0.10 or absolute difference ≤20°C
- Emergency vehicle counting: YOLO-World open-vocabulary detector with class-agnostic NMS

COMPARISON TO EXISTING BENCHMARKS:
- Remote sensing VQA: RSVQA, RSIVQA, HRVQA, VQA-TextRS focus on generic land-cover or RGB-only
- Disaster response VQA: FloodNet (post-flood), RescueNet-VQA (post-disaster) - not wildfire-specific or thermal-grounded
- Wildfire datasets: FLAME 1 (fire classification), FLAME 2 (multi-spectral), FLAME 3 (radiometric thermal) - not formulated as VQA
- WildFireVQA uniquely combines wildfire domain, VQA task structure, and radiometric thermal reasoning

LIMITATIONS:
- Current evaluation provides radiometric statistics as explicit text (RAG) rather than requiring models to infer from thermal inputs directly
- Limited to prescribed burns from three locations - generalization to other fire types and geographies needs validation
- Questions designed for UAV-based monitoring - may not fully cover ground-based or satellite perspectives

APPLICATIONS:
- UAV-based wildfire situational awareness and tactical decision support
- Training and benchmarking vision-language models for safety-critical fire scenarios
- Development of interactive wildfire response systems and digital twins (e.g., FIRETWIN, FIREVLM)
- Operational intelligence for fire behavior assessment, hotspot localization, and flight planning

IMPACT:
- Fills critical gap in wildfire-specific multimodal reasoning benchmarks
- Enables temperature-grounded evaluation beyond visual appearance
- Establishes baseline performance and failure modes for current MLLMs
- Provides open-source dataset and evaluation code for community use

DATASET AVAILABILITY:
Open-source at https://github.com/mobiiin/WildFire_VQA

FUTURE DIRECTIONS:
- End-to-end thermal inference without explicit radiometric text summaries
- Expansion to additional fire types, locations, and environmental conditions
- Integration with physics-grounded fire propagation models
- Multi-turn conversational evaluation for interactive decision support
- Temporal reasoning across video sequences for fire progression tracking

SIGNIFICANCE FOR VLM RESEARCH:
- Demonstrates value and limitations of temperature-grounded reasoning in current MLLMs
- Shows modality-dependent performance gaps (RGB >> Thermal for current models)
- Reveals model-dependent RAG effectiveness (stronger models benefit, weaker degrade)
- Highlights need for improved thermal understanding and cross-modal integration
- Provides safety-critical domain for evaluating real-world MLLM deployment readiness

RELATION TO BROADER WILDFIRE MONITORING:
- Complements sensor networks (IoT), satellite monitoring, and ground surveillance
- Addresses limitations: real-time response, temperature grounding, fine-grained spatial reasoning
- Supports autonomous UAV navigation and decision-making in fire scenarios
- Enables natural language interfaces for wildfire intelligence systems
- Bridges perception, reasoning, and operational decision-making for emergency response
