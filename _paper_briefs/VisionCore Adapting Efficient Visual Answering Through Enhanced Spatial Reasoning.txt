Abstract. Integrating spatially aware instruction tuning with Low-
Rank Adaptation (LoRA) in pre-trained small-scale models unlocks 
enhanced performance in vision-language tasks. Although such models 
excel on general-domain data, they frequently lack the precise spatial 
reasoning and computational eﬃciency observed in large-scale architec-
tures. Despite generating detailed textual descriptions, these models fre-
quently struggle with tasks that require fundamental spatial reasoning 
abilities–such as distinguishing left from right or accurately discerning 
the relative positions of objects within a given context. This work investi-
gates how integrating image-space coordinate annotations from the GQA 
dataset enhances spatial awareness in the Deep Seek 1.3B Visual Lan-
guage Model (VLM). By freezing pre-trained weights and introducing 
specialized, trainable low-rank matrices through LoRA, only an addi-
tional 15 million parameters (1.15% of the overall model) and 60MB of 
memory are required, yielding a 16% performance improvement. Experi-
mental evaluations reveal that this combined approach achieves an accu-
racy of 60.3%, surpassing comparable small-scale models and approach-
ing the performance of large-scale architectures (63.5% accuracy) despite 
utilizing only 1.3B parameters–ﬁve times fewer than its larger counter-
parts. This eﬃciency-performance trade-oﬀ highlights the achievability 
of lightweight adaptation for the capability gap between small- and large-
scale architectures in vision-language tasks. 
1 Introduction 
Human-like visual understanding in vision-language tasks requires spatial 
awareness–the ability to infer object positions, and relationships [ 18]. While large 
vision-language models (VLMs, e.g., 7B+ parameters) excel at spatial reason-
ing, their computational demands hinder real-world deployment [ 3]. Small-scale 
VLMs (1.3B parameters) address eﬃciency but suﬀer from critical limitations:

Recent advances in vision-language models (VLMs) such as MiniGPT-4 [ 29], 
Gemini 1.5 [ 22], and LocVLM-L [ 20] have demonstrated human-like understand-
ing and response capabilities in linguistic tasks, nearly fulﬁlling the demands of 
the Visual Question Answering (VQA) ﬁeld. However, these models continue 
to face notable constraints. For instance, when presented with an image of a 
kitchen scene containing a microwave to the left of a coﬀee maker and asked, ‘Is 
the microwave to the left or right of the coﬀee maker?’, MiniGPT-4 incorrectly 
answers, ‘The microwave is on the right side,’ while Gemini 1.5 generates, ‘The 
microwave is placed above the coﬀee maker.’ In contrast, LocVLM-L correctly 
identiﬁes the spatial relationship: ‘The microwave is positioned to the left of the 
coﬀee maker.’ However, LocVLM-L requires 3× more GPU memory (24GB vs. 
8GB) for inference compared to MiniGPT-4, highlighting its computational inef-
ﬁciency. These challenges underscore a broader issue in the design of large-scale 
language vision models: achieving a balance between computational eﬃciency

and description accuracy. Typically, larger models (with parameters exceed-

by eﬀectively handling complex visual tasks, but this performance comes with 
increased demands for computational power and memory. In contrast, small-scale 
vision-language models oﬀer reduced resource requirements and faster training

gap is crucial for advancing VLMs that are both eﬃcient and capable of robust

The dual challenges of language capability degradation and limited spatial 
reasoning in small-scale VLMs are addressed through parameter-eﬃcient train-
ing (PEFT) with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) [ 
6]. The GQA dataset [ 7]i s 
integrated to enhance standard annotations with spatial metadata–including 
bounding box coordinates (x, y, width, height) and directional relationships (e.g., 
left/right)–thereby injecting explicit spatial priors. LoRA optimizes this process 
by selectively updating low-rank matrices (1.15% of parameters, 15M/1.3B), 
preserving the base model’s pre-trained knowledge while reducing computa-
tional overhead. To further align visual and textual reasoning, structured 
prompts derived from GQA’s scene descriptions are introduced, encoding spatial 
directions (e.g., above/below) and object coordinates. This combined strategy 
achieves 60.3% accuracy on the GQA benchmark–closing 72% of the perfor-
mance gap with 7B, enabling robust spatial understanding with minimal resource 
requirements (2.5 days on 2×V100 GPUs) compared to full-scale retraining.

The Vision Core model, using the Deep Seek-VL 1.3B VLM backbone with 
15M LoRA parameters, was evaluated on GQA and VQAv2. On GQA Test-
Dev, it achieves 60.3%, outperforming larger models (MiniGPT-4 at 43.5% 
and UniﬁedQAv2-2.9B at 42.3%) and approaching state-of-the-art 7B models 
(LLaVA-v1.5 at 62% and LocVLM-L at 63.5%). However, on VQAv2-Val it 
reaches 63.3%, trailing models like Qwen-VL (78.8%), which suggests a trade-oﬀ 
between parameter eﬃciency and generalization. 
5 Conclusion 
This paper introduces the Vision Core model to address two critical challenges in 
computational eﬃciency and limited spatial reasoning. By leveraging the small-
scale DeepSeek 1.3B backbone and integrating LoRA-based parameter-eﬃcient 
training, the model eﬀectively reduces the computational burden while enhancing 
spatial understanding. The approach involves freezing the pre-trained model’s 
layers, except for the 15M LoRA parameters (1.15% of the model), which require 
only 60MB of additional memory. Furthermore, the incorporation of spatial scene 
descriptions derived from the GQA dataset enables the model to develop a more 
holistic understanding of object localization and spatial relationships (e.g., left, 
right, above, and below).

As a result, Vision Core achieves 60.3% accuracy on the GQA dataset, outper-
forming other small-scale models and approaching the performance of 7B-scale 
models such as LocVLM-L (63.5%), despite using 5× fewer parameters. This 
study demonstrates that combining compact architectures, parameter-eﬃcient 
ﬁne-tuning, and spatial scene augmentation can yield competitive results in 
visual question-answering tasks, paving the way for more accessible and scalable 
vision-language solutions, particularly in resource-constrained environments. 
5.1 Limitations 
The limitation of the proposed Vision Core, utilizes LoRA-based ﬁne-tuning, is 
its reduced ﬂexibility in handling diverse input and output instructions. This 
issue arises because the additional parameters, located at the end of the trans-
former layers, are ﬁne-tuned using a single conversation format. Consequently, 
the model becomes overly specialized to that speciﬁc wording and syntax, dimin-
ishing its ability to generalize to alternative or unfamiliar instruction styles. 
Such overﬁtting to a particular instruction pattern may undermine the model’s 
robustness in real-world applications where input variability is common. 
5.2 F uture W ork 
Future research will focus on expanding the model’s adaptability to diverse 
instruction styles by incorporating multi-style prompt augmentation during 
training, which may help mitigate overﬁtting to a single prompt format. Also, 
exploring adaptive ﬁne-tuning strategies that dynamically adjust parameter 
updates based on task complexity could further enhance generalization and per-
formance.
